SPSS Hands-on Practice Guide for Participants

You can guide the participants through these 4 exact exercises during the session:

Exercise 1: Checking Data Normality (Shapiro-Wilk)

  • Goal: Verify if Enzyme_Activity follows a normal distribution.
  • SPSS Menu: Analyze > Descriptive Statistics > Explore...
  • Settings: Move Enzyme_Activity into Dependent List \(\rightarrow\) Click Plots… \(\rightarrow\) Check Normality plots with tests.
  • Expected Result: \(p > 0.05\) (Shapiro-Wilk), confirming parametric tests are appropriate.

Exercise 2: Comparing 2 Groups (Independent Samples \(t\)-Test)

  • Research Question: Does product shelf life differ significantly between Packaging_A and Packaging_B?

  • SPSS Menu: Analyze > Compare Means > Independent-Samples T Test...

  • Settings:

  • Test Variable: Shelf_Life_Days

  • Grouping Variable: Packaging_Type (Define Groups: Packaging_A, Packaging_B)

  • Expected Result: Sig. (2-tailed) \(< 0.001\). Packaging_B yields significantly longer shelf life (\(M \approx 22.5\text{ days}\)) than Packaging_A (\(M \approx 14.0\text{ days}\)).

Exercise 3: Comparing 3+ Groups (One-Way ANOVA & Tukey Post-Hoc)

  • Research Question: Does mean enzyme activity differ significantly across the three academic departments?

  • SPSS Menu: Analyze > Compare Means > One-Way ANOVA...

  • Settings:

  • Dependent List: Enzyme_Activity

  • Factor: Department

  • Post Hoc…: Check Tukey

  • Expected Result: Sig. \(< 0.001\). Tukey post-hoc reveals Applied Chemistry yields significantly higher activity than Microbiology and Food Science.

Exercise 4: Predicting Outcomes (Simple Linear Regression)

  • Research Question: Can reaction temperature predict the percentage yield of the chemical/food process?

  • SPSS Menu: Analyze > Regression > Linear...

  • Settings:

  • Dependent: Yield_Pct

  • Independent(s): Temperature_C

  • Expected Result:

  • Model Summary: \(R^2 \approx 0.81\) (81% of yield variance explained by temperature).

  • Coefficients Table: Regression equation: \(\text{Yield} = 20.0 + 0.65(\text{Temperature})\).
